Bank Accounts

Canadian banks generally offer two basic types of personal accounts. Most people open one of each.

Checking Account (Chequing Account)

Your everyday account, used for direct deposit (payroll), debit card purchases, bill payments, and e-transfers. Usually earns little to no interest. Most banks charge a monthly fee unless you meet certain conditions (minimum balance, direct deposit, or newcomer status).

Savings Account

Meant for holding money you don't need to touch often. Earns interest (rates vary widely by bank and promotion). Some savings accounts limit the number of free withdrawals per month.

As a newcomer, you'll typically open a checking account first to receive your first paycheque, then add a savings account once you have money to set aside.
